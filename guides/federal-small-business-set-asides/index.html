Federal Contracting Guide

Federal small-business set-asides change who can compete. Verify the exact restriction before you pursue.

Set-aside status can make an opportunity more relevant to a qualified small business, but the contractor still has to satisfy current size, program, certification, solicitation, and performance requirements.

A set-aside limits competition for a federal procurement. Some set-asides are available to qualifying small businesses generally; others are connected to SBA contracting programs such as 8(a), HUBZone, Women-Owned Small Business, or Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business. The opportunity notice and current government rules control.

Why set-aside status matters in opportunity matching

If a procurement is restricted to a category your business cannot satisfy, a strong technical capability match may still be irrelevant. If your business does qualify, the set-aside can be an important prioritization signal because the eligible competitive field is defined differently from a full-and-open procurement.

This makes set-aside data valuable in matching—but it should never be treated as a substitute for official eligibility review.

Small-business status depends on the procurement context

SBA size standards determine whether a business qualifies as small for federal contracting purposes, and those standards are connected to industry classifications. Contractors should review the NAICS code and current size standard associated with the procurement rather than assuming that a company is “small” for every federal opportunity.

Socioeconomic programs add their own requirements

Federal contracting includes programs for businesses that meet defined ownership, location, veteran, disadvantaged-business, or other eligibility conditions. Certification and representation rules differ by program and can change.

8(a)

The SBA 8(a) Business Development program has specific eligibility and participation requirements. Verify current SBA guidance.

HUBZone

HUBZone eligibility includes program-specific requirements related to the business and its employees. Use SBA's current certification guidance.

WOSB / EDWOSB

The Women-Owned Small Business Federal Contract program has current certification and industry conditions that should be verified with SBA.

SDVOSB

Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business eligibility and certification should be confirmed using the current federal certification system and procurement notice.

Read the solicitation even when the set-aside looks right

Set-aside eligibility is only one dimension of fit. A contractor must still review scope, required experience, staffing, licenses, clearances, performance location, bonding or insurance, schedule, submission instructions, evaluation factors, clauses, and amendments.

A useful bid/no-bid process asks two different questions: May we compete? and Should we compete? The first is about eligibility and mandatory requirements. The second is about strategic and operational fit.

How RFCP uses set-aside information

The Registered Federal Contractors Portal can use business-profile and opportunity signals to help prioritize notices for review. Set-aside alignment can improve relevance, but APROPOS does not certify program eligibility and does not alter the agency's procurement terms.
