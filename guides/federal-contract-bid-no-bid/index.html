Federal Opportunity Qualification

Make the bid/no-bid decision before the proposal consumes the business.

A relevant notice is not automatically a good pursuit. Review capability, eligibility, resources, timing, competitive position and the actual solicitation requirements before committing proposal effort.

Explore Federal MatchingVerify on SAM.gov
Capability

Can you perform the scope?

Compare the required products, services, geography, staffing, licenses, experience and delivery capacity with what the business can actually provide.

Eligibility

Can you compete for this notice?

Check active registration, size status, set-aside conditions, certifications and every mandatory solicitation requirement.

Execution

Can you respond on time?

Account for questions deadlines, site visits, teaming, pricing, documents, representations and internal review—not only the final due date.

Economics

Should you pursue it?

Consider expected value, proposal cost, margin, contract risk, payment timing and whether the opportunity fits the company's growth strategy.

A no-bid can be a good decision. Qualification protects limited business-development capacity and allows the team to focus on opportunities with stronger evidence of fit.

Use official requirements as the controlling source.

SBA advises businesses to assess readiness, determine whether government buyers purchase what they sell, understand NAICS and size standards, and maintain required SAM registration. RFCP can support prioritization, but the solicitation and issuing agency control the procurement.
