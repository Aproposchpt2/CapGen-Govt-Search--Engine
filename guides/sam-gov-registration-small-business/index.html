Federal Contracting Guide

SAM.gov registration gets your business into the federal award system. It does not make every opportunity a fit.

Understand what active entity registration does, what the Unique Entity ID represents, and what contractors still need to evaluate before deciding to pursue a federal opportunity.

For direct federal awards, registration matters. SAM.gov states that organizations that want to directly bid on government contracts or apply for federal assistance need entity registration. Registration also assigns the organization a Unique Entity ID. Contractors should use SAM.gov itself for registration, renewal, and status questions.

Registration and a Unique Entity ID are related but not identical

SAM.gov lets an organization either complete entity registration or, for certain purposes, request only a Unique Entity ID. A UEI by itself does not mean the organization is registered to directly bid on federal awards. Businesses planning to compete as prime awardees should follow the current SAM.gov Entity Registration instructions.

Active registration is a readiness condition, not a contract recommendation

Being registered tells the federal award system who your entity is and records required representations and information. It does not determine that a particular solicitation matches your capabilities, staffing, past performance, certifications, location, pricing capacity, or risk tolerance.

After registration, the contractor still needs a repeatable opportunity-discovery and qualification process.

Keep registration information current

SAM.gov provides status, renewal, and update functions for registered entities. Contractors should monitor their registration directly in SAM.gov and follow the current renewal instructions rather than relying on an outside platform to determine official status.

Your business profile used for opportunity matching should also reflect current capabilities, NAICS codes, certifications, service area, and operating capacity. If the profile is stale, even a sophisticated matching process can prioritize the wrong opportunities.

What to review after you find an opportunity

Entity and registration

Confirm the registration and representations required by the procurement and your intended role.

NAICS and size

Review the opportunity's assigned NAICS code and the current SBA size standard when small-business status matters.

Set-aside status

Verify whether competition is unrestricted or reserved for a small-business or socioeconomic program.

Solicitation requirements

Read attachments, amendments, instructions, evaluation criteria, clauses, schedule, and submission requirements.

Where the Registered Federal Contractors Portal fits

The Portal is designed for registered federal contractors who want business-profile-based opportunity matching and prioritization. It is a private APROPOS Group LLC service, not a registration service operated by the federal government.

Use SAM.gov for the official registration record and federal opportunity notices. Use APROPOS tools as a decision-support layer for finding and evaluating opportunities that may align with your business.
