Federal Contractor Positioning

Make your capability statement useful to the buyer reading it.

A capability statement should quickly explain what the company does, why it is relevant, what evidence supports that claim, and how a buyer or teaming partner can verify and contact the business.

Match Your CapabilitiesReview SBA Small Business Search
Core Capabilities

Lead with the work you perform

Describe specific products, services and outcomes in language that maps to the buyers and acquisition categories you pursue.

Differentiators

Explain why your business is distinct

Use evidence: specialized expertise, delivery model, licenses, proprietary processes, geographic coverage or measurable performance.

Evidence

Show relevant experience

Use concise past-performance examples or comparable commercial work that demonstrates the ability to deliver similar scope.

Identifiers

Make verification easy

Include current business contact information and relevant identifiers, classifications or certifications when they apply to the audience and pursuit.

Do not turn the document into a biography. A capability statement works best when it is tailored to the market and makes the next conversation easier.

Connect the statement to your federal market strategy.

SBA's contracting guidance recommends assessing what the government buys, understanding NAICS and registration, and using federal contracting assistance when needed. A capability statement should reinforce that same market focus rather than list every service the company could theoretically perform.
