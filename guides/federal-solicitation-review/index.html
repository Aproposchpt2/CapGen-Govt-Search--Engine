Federal Contract Document Review

Read the solicitation package as a set of requirements, not a single document.

Federal opportunities can include notices, amendments, statements of work, instructions, evaluation criteria, clauses, forms and attachments. A complete review tracks how those pieces work together before a response is planned.

Explore Federal MatchingVerify the Official Notice
Notice

Confirm the opportunity record

Verify solicitation number, agency, notice type, set-aside, NAICS, response date, points of contact and amendment history against the official source.

Scope

Understand what must be delivered

Read the statement of work, performance requirements, quantities, locations, schedule and technical constraints before evaluating fit.

Instructions

Build a submission checklist

Capture format, page limits, required volumes, representations, forms, pricing instructions, attachments, signatures and delivery method.

Evaluation

Know how the response will be judged

Separate mandatory requirements from scored factors and understand how technical approach, experience, price or other factors are weighted when stated.

Track amendments as part of the package

An amendment can change dates, answers, specifications, forms or submission instructions. A review process should record each amendment and verify that the response uses the current package.

Do not rely on a summary when preparing a response. RFCP can help organize opportunity intelligence, but contractors should verify the official notice, solicitation files and amendments from the issuing source.

Use the package to drive the bid/no-bid and proposal plan.

Document review should surface compliance requirements, unanswered questions, dependencies and resource demands early enough to affect the pursuit decision—not after proposal drafting has begun.
