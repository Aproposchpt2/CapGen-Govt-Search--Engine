What is federal contract matching? Federal contract matching is a prioritization process. It compares federal opportunities with a contractor's business profile and relevant fit signals so the contractor can focus review on a smaller set of opportunities that appear more applicable. A match does not determine eligibility and does not replace the solicitation or SAM.gov.

SAM.gov is the official source. Matching helps decide what deserves review.

SAM.gov is the federal government's official environment for contract opportunity notices. Contractors can search the federal marketplace there and verify the controlling notice, amendments, attachments, response dates, and agency information. The Registered Federal Contractors Portal does not replace SAM.gov.

The matching problem comes after discovery: a registered contractor still needs to determine which opportunities align with what the business can perform, which set-asides may apply, whether the timing and geography are practical, and whether the solicitation deserves deeper document review. GSA also advises small businesses to search strategically and use available filters when identifying federal opportunities.

How business-profile matching narrows federal opportunities

Business capabilities

Match the products, services, technical work, staffing model, delivery capacity, and business evidence against the work the agency is actually seeking.

NAICS alignment

Use the opportunity's NAICS classification as a market signal, then verify that the statement of work and mandatory requirements genuinely fit the business. A NAICS match alone does not establish qualification.

Set-asides and certifications

Identify whether an opportunity is unrestricted, a small-business set-aside, or tied to an SBA socioeconomic program. Current eligibility must still be verified against the notice and applicable SBA requirements.

Geography and timing

Compare place of performance, site visits, response deadlines, staffing windows, delivery schedules, and other practical constraints with the contractor's operating footprint.

Experience and readiness

Consider past performance, licenses, insurance, bonding, security conditions, financial capacity, staffing, and management readiness before treating a match as pursuit-ready.

Solicitation requirements

Review the actual scope, instructions, evaluation factors, clauses, attachments, and amendments before a final bid/no-bid decision. Matching is the start of qualification, not the end.

Federal contract matching is different from keyword searching

Keyword searching starts with words in a notice. Business-profile matching starts with the contractor. That distinction matters because the same term can appear in an opportunity that is outside the contractor's size status, certifications, geography, experience, staffing capacity, contract type, or required delivery model.

A useful matching workflow therefore combines multiple signals rather than treating any single field as proof of fit. The goal is not to produce the largest list of federal opportunities. The goal is to improve the order in which a contractor reviews them.

From a matched opportunity to a bid/no-bid decision

A matched opportunity should trigger a structured review of mandatory requirements, technical fit, set-aside eligibility, response timing, competitive position, pricing capacity, performance risk, and proposal effort. If a requirement is unclear, the solicitation and issuing agency remain controlling.

Federal contract matching questions

Do I still use SAM.gov?

Yes. SAM.gov remains the official source for federal contract opportunities and controlling notice information. RFCP helps prioritize which public opportunities deserve closer contractor review.

Does a NAICS match mean I qualify?

No. NAICS is one matching signal. Scope, size and set-aside conditions, certifications, licenses, experience, timing, geography, and other requirements must also be verified.

How do set-asides affect matching?

Set-aside information can narrow the pool when an opportunity is reserved for small businesses or an SBA socioeconomic program. The contractor remains responsible for verifying current eligibility.

Does a match mean I should bid?

No. A match means the opportunity appears relevant enough to inspect. A separate bid/no-bid review should determine whether pursuing it is commercially and operationally sensible.

Does RFCP guarantee awards?

No. RFCP does not guarantee eligibility, proposal acceptance, evaluation results, selection, or contract awards.

What happens after matching?

Review the solicitation package, validate mandatory requirements, make a bid/no-bid decision, and use deeper fit analysis when the opportunity warrants additional decision support.
